Arc A750 vs RX 6600: Gaming Performance & Bottleneck (2026)

Expect a small but real lead for the Arc A750: 39 FPS at 1440p High against 37 for the RX 6600 with a Ryzen 7 9800X3D, a 4% gap that is about the same at 4K. It's a cross-brand matchup: Intel's 2022 card against AMD's 2021 card. The order holds at 1080p (4% apart), so the Arc A750 is the quicker card at every resolution. Ray tracing widens the gap to 51% (16 vs 11 FPS at 1440p), as the RX 6600's RT hardware is less efficient. In esports games like CS2 or Valorant at 1080p they reach 191 and 184 FPS, enough for a 144 Hz monitor. At 4K the RX 6600 manages 21 FPS native, and FSR Quality lifts that to about 30. Both carry 8 GB of VRAM, which is already tight at 1440p High in new games. To stay under a 10% CPU bottleneck at 1440p, both need only a Ryzen 5 3600-class CPU.

Arc A750 vs RX 6600: quick answers

Is the Arc A750 faster than the RX 6600?

Yes. The Arc A750 is about 4% faster at 4K and 4% faster at 1440p in AAA games at High settings. With ray tracing on at 1440p the Arc A750 gets about 16 FPS and the RX 6600 11 FPS.

How many FPS do the Arc A750 and RX 6600 get?

With a Ryzen 7 9800X3D at High settings in AAA games, the Arc A750 averages about 60 FPS at 1080p, 39 at 1440p and 22 at 4K. The RX 6600 averages 57, 37 and 21 FPS. DLSS, FSR or XeSS upscaling adds roughly 40% where the graphics card is the limit.

What CPU do I need for the Arc A750 or RX 6600?

To keep the CPU bottleneck under 10% at 1440p, the Arc A750 needs a Ryzen 5 3600 or faster and the RX 6600 needs a Ryzen 5 3600 or faster. At 1080p you need a faster CPU for the same result, and at 4K a slower one is fine. A slower CPU narrows the gap between the Arc A750 and the RX 6600.
