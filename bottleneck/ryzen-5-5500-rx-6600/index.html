Ryzen 5 5500 with RX 6600: Bottleneck & FPS

The Ryzen 5 5500 and RX 6600 leave the CPU with plenty of spare headroom at 1080p: about 57 FPS on average in AAA games at High settings, with 1% lows around 45 FPS. The RX 6600 is the limiting part at every resolution, so the 6-core Ryzen 5 5500 (AM4) still has headroom for about 90 FPS and room for a faster card later. In esports titles such as CS2 or Valorant expect around 181 FPS, enough for a 144 Hz monitor, while CPU-heavy games like Microsoft Flight Simulator drop to about 49 FPS with a 31% CPU bottleneck. Its 8 GB of VRAM is fine at 1080p but gets tight at 4K High. With ray tracing on, expect about 17 FPS at 1080p.

Ryzen 5 5500 + RX 6600: quick answers

Does the Ryzen 5 5500 bottleneck the RX 6600?

1080p: 36% GPU-limited. 1440p: 59% GPU-limited. UW 1440p: 67% GPU-limited. 4K: 77% GPU-limited. At 1080p the CPU keeps up, so this is a sensible pairing. A GPU-limited result is the normal, healthy state for gaming.

How many FPS does the Ryzen 5 5500 with RX 6600 get?

About 57 FPS at 1080p, 37 FPS at 1440p, 30 FPS at UW 1440p, 21 FPS at 4K in AAA games at High settings without upscaling. In esports games like CS2 and Valorant it reaches roughly 181 FPS at 1080p.

What is the best upgrade for a Ryzen 5 5500 + RX 6600 build?

At 1080p, the most effective upgrade is the RTX 5060 or RTX 5050. Use the calculator to check other resolutions and settings.
