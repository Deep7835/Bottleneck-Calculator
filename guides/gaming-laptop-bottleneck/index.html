Gaming laptop bottlenecks: TGP, thermals and CPU vs GPU limits

A gaming laptop's bottleneck works differently from a desktop's. The CPU and GPU share one power budget and one cooling system, the graphics chip is a cut-down version of its desktop namesake, and two laptops with the same GPU can perform very differently. This guide explains what limits a laptop, with numbers for common pairings from our bottleneck calculator.

The short answer

At native resolution and High settings, most gaming laptops are GPU-limited. None of the 10 common pairings below is meaningfully CPU-limited at 1080p High, and all are GPU-limited at 1440p.

Chasing a 144 Hz or 240 Hz panel changes that. Drop to Medium and turn on upscaling, and 8 of the 10 become CPU-limited, some by more than 25%.

The GPU name isn't the whole story. An RTX 5070 Laptop is about 40% slower than a desktop RTX 5070 in our index, and its power limit (TGP) varies by model.

The biggest free fixes: plug in, use the performance mode, and switch the MUX to the dedicated GPU.

The gap isn't constant. The RTX 5060 Laptop is only 10% behind its desktop namesake, while the RTX 5070 Laptop is 40% behind and lands close to the 5060 Laptop, with 8 GB of VRAM instead of 12. TGP: why two laptops with the same GPU perform differently

TGP (total graphics power) is the power limit the laptop maker sets for the graphics chip. NVIDIA allows a wide range for each laptop GPU, so a thin 14-inch laptop and a thick 17-inch one can carry the same RTX 5070 Laptop at very different TGPs. A higher limit lets the GPU hold higher clocks, and the gap between a low-TGP and a high-TGP version of the same chip can be larger than the gap to the next GPU tier.

Many laptops also use Dynamic Boost, which moves power from the CPU to the GPU when the processor has headroom. The advertised maximum often includes that boost, so the GPU only reaches it when the CPU isn't busy. In CPU-heavy games, that's exactly when it can't.

Our laptop numbers assume a well-cooled machine running at full power. Thin laptops with lower TGP limits can be 10–25% slower.

How to check your laptop's TGP

  1. Check the spec sheet. Most makers list the GPU's maximum graphics power on the product page, often with the Dynamic Boost figure included.
  2. Open the NVIDIA Control Panel and click System Information at the bottom left. On most laptops it lists the GPU's maximum graphics power.
  3. Measure it under load. Run a game, then check the GPU power reading in HWiNFO or GPU-Z. If it never gets close to the spec, the laptop is in a quiet mode, on battery or running too hot.
  4. Read reviews of the exact model. The same laptop line can ship with different TGPs and coolers across sizes and years.

Laptop CPU tiers: HX, H and U

Laptop processors come in power classes, and the class tells you more than the brand or the core count.

  • HX chips are desktop-class silicon in a laptop package, with high power limits. They're found in larger gaming laptops and come closest to desktop gaming performance.
  • H and HS chips are mainstream laptop processors with moderate power limits, common in thinner gaming laptops. AMD's Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 belongs closer to this group despite its name.
  • U chips are low-power processors for thin-and-light laptops. They can run a light game, but they hold back any real gaming GPU.

Why 1080p high-refresh laptops run into the CPU

Lower settings and upscaling cut the GPU's work per frame, but the CPU's work per frame stays roughly the same. Push for 165 or 240 FPS and the processor becomes the limit long before the graphics chip does. In CS2-style esports at 1080p, a Core Ultra 9 275HX with an RTX 5080 Laptop is 11% CPU-limited at about 379 FPS, while a Ryzen 7 7840HS with an RTX 4060 Laptop is still GPU-limited at 217. In CPU-heavy strategy and simulation games, even the 4060 laptops are CPU-limited at 1080p (about 59 FPS with the 7840HS and 67 with the Core i7-13650HX).

A laptop adds a second limit our model doesn't capture: shared power and heat. When the GPU is working hard, the CPU often gets less power and lower clocks than it would on its own, which can make CPU-bound games worse on a hot or thin laptop. If GPU usage sits well below 90% while the frame rate stays below your refresh rate, the processor or its power limit is the bottleneck. How to get full performance from a gaming laptop

  1. Plug in. On battery, laptops cut GPU and CPU power sharply and often cap the frame rate. Expect a much lower result.
  2. Use the performance mode in the maker's control app. Quiet and balanced modes lower power limits for both chips.
  3. Switch the MUX to the dedicated GPU. In hybrid mode, frames pass through the integrated graphics on the way to the screen, which costs performance, most of all at high frame rates. A MUX switch (often labeled dGPU or discrete mode) wires the GPU straight to the panel, usually after a restart. Laptops with NVIDIA Advanced Optimus switch automatically. Without a MUX, an external monitor connected to a port wired to the dedicated GPU gets the same benefit on many laptops.
  4. Give it air. Use it on a hard surface, keep the vents clear and clean dust from the fans. A cooling pad or simply raising the back a few centimeters lowers temperatures by a few degrees, which can mean steadier clocks.
  5. Try a GPU undervolt. Lowering the GPU's voltage curve with a tool such as MSI Afterburner can keep clocks the same at less power and heat. Go in small steps and test for stability. CPU undervolting is locked on many recent laptops.
  6. Cap the frame rate just under your refresh rate when you're CPU-limited. It smooths frame pacing and keeps the laptop cooler.

Frequently asked questions

Is a laptop RTX 5070 as fast as a desktop RTX 5070?

No. In our index the RTX 5070 Laptop GPU is about 40% slower than the desktop RTX 5070 and has 8 GB of VRAM instead of 12 GB. It performs closer to an RTX 5060 Laptop. Its exact speed also depends on the TGP the laptop maker chose.

What is TGP on a gaming laptop?

TGP (total graphics power) is the power limit the laptop maker sets for the graphics chip. The same GPU can run at very different TGPs in different laptops, and a higher TGP means higher sustained clocks. Check it on the spec sheet or in the NVIDIA Control Panel under System Information.

Do gaming laptops have CPU bottlenecks?

At native 1080p or 1440p with High settings, most are GPU-limited. At 1080p with Medium settings and upscaling, which is how people chase 144–240 Hz, most common pairings become CPU-limited, especially with H-series processors. An HX processor holds up better.

Is an HX processor worth it in a gaming laptop?

For high-refresh gaming, yes. In our model an Intel Core i7-14650HX has about 20% more CPU headroom than a Ryzen 7 8845HS. At native resolution with High settings the GPU usually sets the frame rate, so the difference matters less there.

Does a MUX switch improve gaming performance?

Yes. In hybrid mode, frames go through the integrated graphics before reaching the screen, which costs performance, most of all at high frame rates. Switching the MUX to the dedicated GPU, or using a laptop with Advanced Optimus, removes that step.

Why is my gaming laptop slower on battery?

Laptops cut CPU and GPU power sharply on battery to protect battery life, and many also cap the frame rate. Plug in and select the maker's performance mode for full speed.
