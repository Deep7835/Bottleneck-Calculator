Mac vs gaming PC: what the same money buys for gaming

Apple Silicon Macs can play games, and the faster ones play them well. But for the same money a gaming PC is usually much faster, and it runs every Windows game. This guide puts numbers on that gap, using our Mac gaming calculator for the Macs and the same model as our bottleneck calculator for the PCs, and explains which games a Mac can and can't run.

The short answer

For gaming alone, buy a PC. At every price tier below, the PC is faster in every game we compare, often two to three times faster, and it runs games a Mac can't.

If you need a Mac anyway, it can be your gaming machine too, as long as your games have native Mac versions or run well through translation, and you can live without games that use kernel anti-cheat.

For laptops, the gap is smaller but still there. A mid-range gaming laptop outruns every MacBook Air and base MacBook Pro in games. MacBooks win on battery life, noise and weight.

Two more costs to count on the Mac side: Apple charges a lot to upgrade memory and storage at purchase, and neither can be added later. A PC's graphics card, memory and storage can all be upgraded in a few minutes.

What PC is a Mac equal to?

For native Mac games, our model matches each Mac's graphics to the closest desktop card. These are rough equivalents for gaming only; Macs are far stronger than these cards suggest at video editing and other creative work.

  • MacBook Air 13″ M4 (8-core GPU): about an RX 6400.
  • Mac mini M4: about an RX 6500 XT.
  • MacBook Pro 14″ M5: about an RTX 3050 8GB.
  • Mac mini M4 Pro (20-core GPU): about an RTX 3060 8GB.
  • Mac Studio M4 Max (32-core GPU): about an RTX 5060.
  • Mac Studio M4 Max (40-core GPU): about an RTX 5060 Ti 8GB.

The fastest chip here, the M4 Max with a 40-core GPU, lands near an RTX 5060 Ti 8GB, a mid-range PC card. None of these reaches an RTX 5070; only the far pricier Mac Studio M3 Ultra (80-core GPU) gets into that class, near an RX 9070. The processors are a different story: Apple's CPU cores are fast, and in CPU-heavy games like Baldur's Gate 3 the Macs hold up better than in graphics-heavy ones. Native, translated and blocked games

The game library matters more than raw speed. A Windows game reaches a Mac in one of three ways.

Native Mac versions

These run best. Our calculator covers Cyberpunk 2077, Baldur's Gate 3, Resident Evil 4, Assassin's Creed Shadows, Death Stranding Director's Cut, No Man's Sky, Lies of P, World of Warcraft, League of Legends and Minecraft (Java), all with native Apple Silicon versions. Many native ports support MetalFX, Apple's upscaler, which works much like DLSS or FSR.

Translated: CrossOver and the Game Porting Toolkit

CrossOver, and Apple's Game Porting Toolkit that it builds on, translate Windows games and DirectX graphics so they run on macOS. Many work, but translation costs performance. In our model the same AAA game runs about 27% slower translated than native on the Mac mini M4 Pro (42 vs 58 FPS at 1080p) and 28% slower on the M4 Max Mac Studio. Our list includes Counter-Strike 2, Elden Ring and GTA V (story mode). Elden Ring is locked to 60 FPS by the game, which the M4 Max reaches.

Blocked: kernel anti-cheat

Some of the most played games won't run on a Mac at all. Valorant's Vanguard and Call of Duty's Ricochet anti-cheat don't run on macOS, so Valorant and Warzone are out, and GTA Online blocks the translated version that runs GTA V's story mode. Fortnite has no macOS version. The workaround is cloud gaming: GeForce NOW and Xbox Cloud Gaming stream the game from a PC, so it runs on any Mac with a fast connection, with a little added input lag and a monthly fee for the better tiers.

If your main game is a competitive shooter, check that it runs on macOS before anything else. That alone settles the question for many players.

Unified memory as video memory

Macs don't have separate video memory. The CPU and GPU share one pool of unified memory, and macOS lets the GPU use about two-thirds of it (three-quarters from 48 GB up). In our model that works out to:

  • 16 GB Mac: about 10.4 GB for graphics.
  • 24 GB Mac: about 15.6 GB for graphics.
  • 36 GB Mac: about 23.4 GB for graphics.
  • 48 GB Mac: about 36 GB for graphics.

So a 16 GB Mac has more room for graphics than an 8 GB graphics card, and a 36 GB M4 Max more than a 16 GB card. The catch is that the game itself, macOS and your other apps come out of the same pool, whereas a PC with 16 GB of RAM and an 8 GB card has 24 GB in total. In practice, video memory is rarely what holds a Mac back in games; GPU speed is.

Avoid 8 GB Macs for gaming. Modern games swap to the SSD, and in our model the Mac mini M4's 1% lows drop from 24 to 15 FPS with 8 GB, which you feel as stutter. Base models from the M4 generation on start at 16 GB.

Who should buy which

  • Buy a gaming PC if gaming is the main reason for the purchase, you play competitive shooters with anti-cheat, you want new Windows releases on day one, or you want to upgrade later. Our balanced build guide shows how to plan one.
  • Buy a Mac if you need macOS for work or study, mostly play native titles such as Baldur's Gate 3, World of Warcraft, League of Legends or Minecraft, and can use cloud gaming for the rest. Choose at least 16 GB of memory, and a Pro or Max chip if games matter.
  • Buy a MacBook over a gaming laptop if you'll carry it every day and game occasionally. Buy the gaming laptop if you game most days and mostly play plugged in.
  • Consider both: a Mac for work plus a budget gaming PC or a console often costs about the same as a high-end Mac, and plays far more games.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Mac or a PC better for gaming?

A PC. At similar prices a gaming PC is usually two to three times faster in games and runs titles a Mac can't, such as Valorant and Warzone. In Cyberpunk 2077 at 1080p High, a Mac mini M4 gets about 31 FPS and a budget PC with an Arc B580 about 77.

What PC graphics card is a Mac equal to?

In native games, our model puts the Mac mini M4 Pro with a 20-core GPU near an RTX 3060 8GB, the M4 Max with a 32-core GPU near an RTX 5060 and the 40-core M4 Max near an RTX 5060 Ti 8GB. Only the far more expensive M3 Ultra Mac Studio reaches RTX 5070 class. Translated Windows games run slower still.

Can you play Valorant, Warzone or Fortnite on a Mac?

Not directly. Valorant's Vanguard and Call of Duty's Ricochet anti-cheat don't run on macOS, and Fortnite has no macOS version. All three can be played through cloud gaming services such as GeForce NOW or Xbox Cloud Gaming, which stream the game from a PC.

How much VRAM does a Mac have?

Macs share unified memory between the CPU and GPU. macOS lets the GPU use about two-thirds of it, so a 16 GB Mac has about 10.4 GB for graphics and a 36 GB Mac about 23.4 GB. That memory also holds the game and macOS, so it isn't directly comparable to a graphics card's VRAM.

Is a MacBook good for gaming?

For native and light games, yes; for demanding games, a gaming laptop is much faster. In Cyberpunk 2077 at 1080p a MacBook Pro M5 gets about 43 FPS and an RTX 5060 gaming laptop about 77. MacBooks win on battery life, noise and weight.

How much slower are Windows games on a Mac through CrossOver?

In our model, a Windows game translated through CrossOver or the Game Porting Toolkit runs about 27–28% slower than a native Mac version on Apple Silicon, and some games don't run at all because of anti-cheat.
