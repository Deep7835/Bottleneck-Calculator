Best CPU for the RTX 5060 and RTX 4060

The RTX 5060 and RTX 4060 are 1080p cards, and they need far less processor than most build guides suggest. Spend too much on the CPU and you've taken money away from the part that actually sets your frame rate. Here's how the popular budget and mainstream chips pair with each card, calculated with the same model as our bottleneck calculator.

Quick picks

Best value: Ryzen 5 5600 or Core i5-12400F. Both keep an RTX 5060 GPU-limited at 1080p, within 3 FPS of a Ryzen 5 7600 in AAA games, on cheap DDR4 platforms.

Cheapest that works: Core i3-12100F. Balanced with the RTX 5060 at 1080p High. It runs out of steam in simulators, strategy games and when streaming, so treat it as a tight-budget pick.

For esports and CPU-heavy games: Core i5-13400F or 14400F. A little faster than the 12400F in simulators and MMOs, and balanced with the card in those games at 1440p.

For an upgrade path: Ryzen 5 7600. No faster with these cards today, but AM5 and the 7600 are ready for an RTX 5070-class card later.

Already on AM4? A Ryzen 5 5600, or a Ryzen 7 5700X3D if you play CPU-heavy games, is a drop-in upgrade for a Ryzen 1000, 2000 or 3000 chip.

What "enough CPU" means at 1080p

Every frame needs work from both parts. The processor prepares it (game logic, physics, draw calls) and the graphics card renders it. A CPU is "enough" when it can prepare frames faster than the card can draw them, because then the card sets the pace and you're getting everything you paid for.

In AAA games at 1080p High, an RTX 5060 can render about 94 FPS and an RTX 4060 about 76. The 5060 is roughly 24% faster. So the bar for a CPU is about 94 FPS for the 5060 and 76 for the 4060. A Core i3-12100F can prepare about 100 FPS in the same games, a Ryzen 5 5600 about 106 and a Ryzen 5 7600 about 136. All three clear the bar, which is why they all land within a few frames of each other.

Two things raise the bar. Lower settings and upscaling make the card faster, so the processor has to keep up with a higher frame rate. And some games, such as simulators, strategy titles and MMOs, simply need far more CPU per frame. Both are covered below.

Esports

Competitive games at 1080p push the frame rate into the hundreds, and the card tops out around 302 FPS. Every current CPU here clears 240 FPS in CS2-style games, from about 252 on the 12100F to 293 on the 7600. The older 3600 manages about 216. None of these builds will drive a 360 Hz monitor, and that's down to the graphics card, not the processor.

Simulators, strategy and MMOs

This is where the CPU you pick shows. At 1080p every chip is the limit, and the Ryzen 5 7600 runs about 36% faster than the Core i3-12100F (75 vs 55 FPS). The Core i5-13400F, Core i5-14400F, Ryzen 7 5700X3D and 7600 are all balanced with the card at 1440p in these games, while the 12100F stays CPU-bound. If you mostly play Microsoft Flight Simulator, Cities: Skylines II or a busy MMO, buy one of those four.

Medium settings and DLSS

To get closer to 144 Hz in AAA games, most people lower settings or turn on DLSS, and that raises the bar for the CPU. With DLSS Quality at 1080p, the Core i3-12100F becomes 24% CPU-limited at about 99 FPS and the Ryzen 5 5600 20% CPU-limited, while the Core i5-12400F (114 FPS) and Ryzen 5 7600 (123 FPS) stay balanced. Dropping to Medium has a similar effect. So if you chase high frame rates rather than image quality, a 12400F or better is worth the small step up.

8 GB of VRAM, 1440p and ray tracing

Both cards have 8 GB of video memory, and that matters more than the CPU for how they age. At 1080p High, modern games fit in about 8 GB. At 1080p Ultra they want around 10 GB, and the overflow costs real performance: with a Ryzen 5 5600 the RTX 5060 drops to about 68 FPS at Ultra and the 4060 to 55, while an RTX 5060 Ti 16GB with 16 GB holds 85. The fix is easy: set textures to High instead of Ultra. The rest of the Ultra settings are usually fine.

1440p is possible but not native. At 1440p High the RTX 5060 averages about 61 FPS and needs about 9.5 GB, slightly more than it has. With DLSS Quality it reaches about 86 FPS with a 5600 and 84 with a 12100F, both GPU-limited. If 1440p is your main resolution, a 16 GB card like the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB or RX 9060 XT 16GB is the better buy.

Ray tracing runs at about 51 FPS at 1080p natively on the 5060 and 71 with DLSS Quality. It's usable with upscaling, and the CPU barely matters for it, since the graphics card does the heavy lifting.

When a cheaper CPU is the smarter buy

On a fixed budget, money moved from the processor to the graphics card usually buys more frames. In AAA games at 1080p a Ryzen 5 7600 + RTX 5060 gets about 94 FPS. A Ryzen 5 5600 + RTX 5060 Ti 16GB gets 99 and is balanced, and a Core i5-12400F + RTX 5060 Ti 16GB gets 105, with twice the VRAM in both cases. AM4 and DDR4 Intel boards and memory cost less than AM5 with DDR5, and that saving is often what pays for the better card.

The faster CPU is the smarter buy when:

  • You play CPU-heavy games. Simulators and strategy games gain 36% from a 7600 over a 12100F, more than any budget GPU upgrade would add.
  • You stream with the x264 encoder. Our model puts the 12100F at 26% CPU-limited while encoding, against 14% GPU-limited for the 14400F. Using NVENC, the card's hardware encoder, avoids most of that cost on any CPU.
  • You'll upgrade the graphics card soon. With an RTX 5070 at 1080p, the 12100F would be 31% CPU-limited and the 5600 27% CPU-limited, while the 7600 stays balanced. At 1440p none of the three holds a 5070 back, so this only matters if you stay at 1080p.

Pairing with an older CPU

An RTX 5060 is a popular upgrade for an older PC. How it pairs at 1080p High in AAA games:

  • Core i7-8700K: balanced, about 85 FPS.
  • Core i5-10400F: balanced, about 83 FPS.
  • Core i5-9400F: 19% CPU-limited, about 74 FPS.
  • Ryzen 5 2600: 30% CPU-limited, about 66 FPS.
  • Ryzen 5 1600: 41% CPU-limited, about 56 FPS.

Six-core Intel chips from the 8th generation onward are close to fine. First- and second-generation Ryzen are where the card is clearly held back. The good news for AM4 owners is that most B450 and X470 boards, and many older ones, accept a Ryzen 5 5600 or 5700X3D after a BIOS update. Our best AM4 CPU guide covers the checks. RAM, power supply and PCIe

Memory. 16 GB in two sticks is the minimum for gaming in 2026, and 32 GB is a cheap way to stop worrying. In our model 16 GB keeps a Ryzen 5 5600 and RTX 5060 balanced, but 8 GB makes the CPU the limit and drops the 1% lows from 69 to 52 FPS, which you'll feel as stutter. DDR4-3200 or 3600 is fine for the 5600, 5700X3D and DDR4 Intel boards. For the 7600, DDR5-6000 is the sweet spot.

Power supply. NVIDIA recommends a 550 W supply for a system with either card. The RTX 4060 is rated at 115 W and the 5060 at 145 W, and both use a single 8-pin connector, so a good 550 to 650 W unit leaves room for a bigger card later.

PCIe x8. Both cards use eight PCIe lanes instead of sixteen. On a PCIe 4.0 or 5.0 board that makes no difference. On a PCIe 3.0 board, such as B450, A520 or a 10th-gen Intel board, the link has half the bandwidth. The loss is small while the game fits in VRAM, but it grows when the 8 GB overflows, which is one more reason to keep textures at High. The Ryzen 5 5500, 5600G and 5700G also limit the slot to PCIe 3.0, so skip them for these cards.

Cooling. The 12100F, 12400F, 13400F, 14400F, Ryzen 5 5600 and Ryzen 5 7600 all ship with a cooler that's adequate for gaming. The 5700X3D doesn't include one.

Frequently asked questions

Will an i3-12100F bottleneck an RTX 5060?

Not at 1080p High in AAA games: the two are balanced at about 89 FPS. The 12100F becomes the limit with DLSS or Medium settings (24% CPU-limited with DLSS Quality) and in CPU-heavy games like simulators.

Is the Ryzen 5 5600 good for an RTX 5060?

Yes. It's GPU-limited at about 90 FPS at 1080p High, within 3 FPS of a Ryzen 5 7600. It also suits 1440p with DLSS Quality at about 86 FPS.

Will a Ryzen 5 3600 bottleneck an RTX 4060 or 5060?

Not the RTX 4060, which is balanced with it at 1080p. With the RTX 5060 the 3600 is 11% CPU-limited, a minor bottleneck at about 81 FPS. A Ryzen 5 5600 drop-in removes it.

Is the Ryzen 7 7800X3D overkill for an RTX 5060?

In most games, yes. It gets the same 94 FPS as a Ryzen 5 7600 at 1080p High because the card is the limit. It only pulls ahead in CPU-heavy games, where it reaches about 95 FPS against 75.

Can the RTX 5060 run games at 1440p?

With upscaling, yes. Natively at 1440p High it averages about 61 FPS and runs slightly short of VRAM. With DLSS Quality it reaches about 86 FPS with a Ryzen 5 5600.

Does the RTX 5060 need a better CPU than the RTX 4060?

Slightly. The 5060 is about 24% faster, so it needs a CPU that can prepare about 94 FPS in AAA games instead of 76. Any current six-core, or even a Core i3-12100F, clears that at 1080p High.
