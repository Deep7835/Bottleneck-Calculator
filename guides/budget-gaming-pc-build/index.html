Budget gaming PC build: a $700 part list with FPS and bottleneck numbers

This is a concrete version of our balanced PC build guide for the cheapest tier worth building new: a 1080p gaming PC around $700. You get a part list, four CPU and graphics card combinations with FPS and bottleneck figures from the same model as our bottleneck calculator, and an upgrade path. Every FPS figure assumes 16 GB of RAM, since that's what fits the budget.

Put together, the cheapest of the four builds below comes to about $670–900, depending on where prices sit when you buy, and the version with a 16 GB card to about $840–1,140. So $700 is the floor for a new 1080p gaming PC in late 2026, not the middle. If memory or graphics card prices drop, the GeForce and Radeon versions fall back toward it too.

A few choices are fixed whatever you pick. Use two memory sticks, not one, so the memory runs in dual channel. Turn on XMP or EXPO in the BIOS so it runs at its rated speed. And buy the power supply from a brand with a good reputation: it's the one part that can take others with it when it fails.

All four are GPU-limited at 1080p, which is the healthy result. A GPU-limited figure with a large percentage means the processor has spare capacity, and that spare capacity is your upgrade path: a faster card later will use it.

  • Cheapest: Ryzen 5 5600 + Arc B580. About 77 FPS at 1080p High, and 12 GB of video memory for the lowest price. It's the build that gets closest to $700.
  • GeForce: Core i5-12400F + RTX 5060. About 88 FPS, 15% faster than the B580 build, with DLSS 4 and the best upscaling support in new games. The trade-off is 8 GB of VRAM.
  • AM5: Ryzen 5 7500F + RX 9060 XT 8GB. About 97 FPS, on the platform with the longest CPU upgrade path.
  • AM5 with a 16 GB card: Ryzen 5 7500F + RX 9060 XT 16GB. About 99 FPS. Barely faster than the 8 GB version at High, but the extra memory matters at Ultra and at 1440p (see below). If you can stretch the budget, this is the one that ages best.

In esports, every build clears 200 FPS, from about 235 to 301. Three of the four reach 240 FPS, and none gets near 360, so a 144 Hz or 165 Hz monitor is the sensible match for this tier.

In AAA games the CPU barely matters at this tier. Every combination is GPU-limited, and with the RX 9060 XT 16GB the gap from the Ryzen 5 5600 to the Ryzen 5 7500F is about 6 FPS. The Ryzen 5 7500F is essentially a Ryzen 5 7600 without integrated graphics, and in our model it lands within a frame of it, so there's no reason to pay more for the 7600 in a build with a graphics card.

CPU-heavy games are different. There every chip is the limit, and the Ryzen 5 7500F runs about 26% faster than the Ryzen 5 5600 (71 vs 56 FPS), with the Core i5-12400F in between at 63. If you mostly play Microsoft Flight Simulator, Cities: Skylines II or a busy MMO, spend the extra on the 7500F.

How to choose:

  • Ryzen 5 5600 if the total price matters most. AM4 boards and DDR4 are the cheapest platform, and the money saved can go to the graphics card.
  • Core i5-12400F if you find a good DDR4 B760 board deal. It's a little quicker than the 5600 in CPU-heavy games, but the LGA1700 socket has no faster chips coming.
  • Ryzen 5 7500F if you'll upgrade later. AM5 takes faster Ryzen chips, including the X3D models, without a new board.

The head-to-heads go deeper: Core i5-12400F vs Ryzen 5 5600, Ryzen 5 7600 vs Ryzen 5 5600 and Ryzen 5 7600 vs Ryzen 5 7500F. For more CPUs with these cards, see the best CPU for the RTX 5060.

Which graphics card: B580, RTX 5060 or RX 9060 XT

The graphics card sets the frame rate here, so it's where the choice matters. With a Ryzen 5 7500F at 1080p:

  • Arc B580 (12 GB, about $250–300): 78 FPS at High, 63 at Ultra.
  • RTX 5060 (8 GB, about $320–420): 89 FPS at High, 65 at Ultra.
  • RX 9060 XT 8GB (8 GB, about $320–420): 97 FPS at High, 71 at Ultra.
  • RX 9060 XT 16GB (16 GB, about $380–470): 99 FPS at High, 81 at Ultra.

Video memory decides how the cards age. At 1080p High, modern games fit in about 8 GB. At Ultra they want around 10 GB, and the 8 GB cards slow down. The two RX 9060 XT versions show it best: the 16 GB card is only 2% faster at High but 13% faster at Ultra. With an 8 GB card, set textures to High rather than Ultra and you avoid most of the loss. Our guide on whether 8 GB of VRAM is enough covers it in detail.

The Arc B580 is the value pick: 12 GB for the least money, though the RTX 5060 is about 14% faster at 1080p High in our model. Intel's Arc cards need Resizable BAR switched on in the BIOS, and they've been reported to lose more performance than rival cards when paired with older, slower CPUs, so they suit a new build better than an old PC.

The RTX 5060 has the widest upscaling support (DLSS 4) and the best ray tracing at this price. Ray tracing at 1080p runs at about 48 FPS natively and 69 with DLSS Quality, so it's usable with upscaling on.

The RX 9060 XT 16GB is the fastest of the four and the safest bet for the next few years, if the budget stretches. Our DLSS vs FSR vs XeSS guide compares the upscalers, and the head-to-heads are here: RTX 5060 vs Arc B580, RX 9060 XT 8GB vs RTX 5060, RX 9060 XT 16GB vs RX 9060 XT 8GB and RTX 5060 vs RTX 4060.

Can a $700 PC play at 1440p?

With upscaling, yes. Natively at 1440p High these builds run at about 55–71 FPS, and the 8 GB cards run slightly short of video memory (games want about 9.5 GB). With DLSS, FSR or XeSS on Quality, they reach about 76–98 FPS, with the 7500F and RX 9060 XT 16GB at the top.

Upscaling makes the card faster, so the processor has to keep up with more frames. At 1440p with Quality upscaling the Ryzen 5 7500F builds are still GPU-limited, and with the 16 GB card even a Ryzen 5 5600 is balanced.

If 1440p is your main resolution, though, a 12 or 16 GB card is the better buy, and the next tier up makes more sense. Our 1440p gaming PC build picks up from here.

The upgrade path

A budget build is a starting point. The graphics card is the usual first upgrade, and the platform decides how far the rest can go.

Graphics card first

Every CPU here has headroom. Add an RTX 5070 later and the Ryzen 5 7500F is 21% GPU-limited at 1440p, about 100 FPS, while the Ryzen 5 5600 is balanced, about 93. At 1080p the picture changes: the 7500F is 11% CPU-limited with that card, a minor bottleneck, but the 5600 is 30% CPU-limited. So if you plan to keep a 1080p monitor and buy a much faster card, AM5 is the better start. A bigger card will also need more power: NVIDIA recommends a 650 W supply for an RTX 5070, so buying 650 W now saves replacing it.

Then the processor

On AM5, the Ryzen 5 7500F can be swapped for any faster Ryzen chip, including the Ryzen 7 7800X3D or Ryzen 7 9800X3D, after a BIOS update. On AM4, the last big step is a Ryzen 7 5700X3D: it raises CPU-heavy games from 56 to 70 FPS in our model and cuts the 5600's bottleneck with an RTX 5070 at 1080p to 13% CPU-limited. Our best AM4 CPU guide covers the checks. The Core i5-12400F can move to a 13th- or 14th-gen Core i5 on the same board, which is a smaller step.

Memory

16 GB is the budget compromise. It keeps a Ryzen 5 5600 and RTX 5060 GPU-limited at 1080p, but some new games and a browser in the background can push past it. When memory prices settle, adding a second pair to reach 32 GB is a cheap fix if the board has four slots. Going down to 8 GB is not an option: in our model it drops the 1% lows from 66 to 52 FPS, which you'd feel as stutter.

Frequently asked questions

Can you build a gaming PC for $700 in 2026?

Yes, but it's close to the floor. At approximate US street prices in October 2026, a Ryzen 5 5600 and Arc B580 build costs about $670–900 and averages about 77 FPS at 1080p High in AAA games. Memory and graphics card prices rose through 2026, so the RTX 5060 and RX 9060 XT versions cost more.

What is the best CPU for a budget gaming PC?

For AAA games at 1080p, a Ryzen 5 5600, Core i5-12400F or Ryzen 5 7500F. All three keep an RTX 5060, RX 9060 XT or Arc B580 GPU-limited. The Ryzen 5 7500F is about 26% faster in CPU-heavy games and sits on AM5, which has the best upgrade path.

Is the RTX 5060 or RX 9060 XT better for a budget build?

They're close. With a Ryzen 5 7500F at 1080p High the RTX 5060 averages about 89 FPS and the RX 9060 XT 8GB about 97. The RTX 5060 has DLSS 4 and stronger ray tracing. The RX 9060 XT 16GB is the better long-term buy, because 16 GB of video memory holds up at Ultra settings and 1440p.

Is the Arc B580 good for a budget gaming PC?

Yes, as the cheapest option. It has 12 GB of video memory and averages about 77 FPS at 1080p High with a Ryzen 5 5600. The RTX 5060 is about 14% faster. Arc cards need Resizable BAR enabled and work best with a modern CPU.

Is 16 GB of RAM enough for a budget gaming PC?

Yes, in two 8 GB sticks. It keeps a Ryzen 5 5600 and RTX 5060 GPU-limited at 1080p. 32 GB is better for new games and multitasking, but memory prices rose sharply from late 2025, so 16 GB is the sensible compromise at this budget. 8 GB causes stutter in modern games.

Can a budget gaming PC run games at 1440p?

With upscaling, yes. At 1440p High these builds average about 55–71 FPS natively and about 76–98 FPS with DLSS, FSR or XeSS on Quality. For 1440p as the main resolution, a 16 GB card is the safer choice.
