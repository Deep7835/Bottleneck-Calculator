1440p gaming PC build: a $1,200 part list with FPS and bottleneck numbers

1440p is where a mid-range PC makes the most sense: sharp enough to see the difference from 1080p, light enough that a mid-range card runs it at high frame rates. This guide turns our balanced PC build guide into a concrete part list for about $1,200, compares five CPU and graphics card combinations, and shows when a faster processor is worth the money. Figures come from the same model as our bottleneck calculator.

With 32 GB of memory, the Ryzen 5 7600 + RTX 5070 version comes to about $1,270–1,730. To hit $1,200, start with 16 GB (2 × 8 GB), which brings it to about $1,200–1,580. In our model 16 GB costs less than one FPS on average at 1440p with this pair, and you can add a second pair later when prices settle. The RX 9070 XT versions cost about $100–120 more.

The basics are the same as any build: two memory sticks, EXPO turned on in the BIOS, and a power supply from a reputable brand. A B650 or B850 board is plenty; pricier boards add connectivity, not frame rate.

At native 1440p every build is GPU-limited, from about 100 FPS with the RTX 5070 to 121 with the RX 9070 XT and the X3D chip. That's what you want at this resolution: the money you put into the card turns straight into frames.

  • RTX 5070 or RX 9070? The RX 9070 is about 4% faster in raster and has 16 GB instead of 12. The RTX 5070 is faster with ray tracing (82 vs 73 FPS with upscaling) and has DLSS 4. See RTX 5070 vs RX 9070.
  • The RX 9070 XT is about 17% faster than the 5070 at native 1440p and matches the RTX 5070 Ti in raster in our model (117 vs 118 FPS), usually for less money. The 5070 Ti keeps the lead in ray tracing, at 97 FPS. Compare RX 9070 XT vs RX 9070, RX 9070 XT vs RTX 5070 and RTX 5070 Ti vs RX 9070 XT.
  • Esports runs at 308 FPS or more on every build, so any of them suits a 240 Hz monitor. Only the X3D build reaches 360 FPS, at about 372.

The monitor and settings to pair it with

These builds average roughly 100–121 FPS in AAA games at native 1440p and 127–153 with upscaling, so a 1440p monitor at 144 to 180 Hz is the natural match. Make sure it supports variable refresh (FreeSync or G-Sync Compatible): it keeps motion smooth when the frame rate sits below the refresh rate, which it will in most new games. A 240 Hz or faster panel only pays off if you mainly play competitive games. Our guide to what counts as a good FPS covers the targets.

Use High rather than Ultra. Ultra costs about 17% of the frame rate with the RX 9070 XT in our model (97 vs 117 FPS) for a difference that's hard to spot in motion. Keep textures high if the card has the memory, since they cost little speed.

Use upscaling on Quality in demanding games. At 1440p, DLSS and FSR Quality render at a lower internal resolution and reconstruct the image, and the result holds up well in most games. It's what makes ray tracing practical here: the RTX 5070 runs ray-traced games at about 59 FPS natively and 82 with DLSS Quality.

Treat frame generation as a bonus. DLSS 4 Multi Frame Generation (RTX 50 only) and AMD's frame generation multiply the displayed frame rate, but the game still responds at the rendered rate. Turn it on when the base frame rate is already around 60 or more. Our DLSS vs FSR vs XeSS guide explains the modes.

Why the graphics card gets the money

At 1440p the graphics card sets the frame rate in most games, so the same extra money buys more there. Moving from the Ryzen 5 7600 to the Ryzen 5 7600X3D costs about $110–120 more, and so does moving from the RTX 5070 to the RX 9070 XT. Spend it on the CPU and the 5070 build gains 1% at native 1440p (100 to 101 FPS), because the card was already the limit. Spend it on the card and you get 117 FPS, 16% more than the X3D with the 5070.

Video memory is the other reason to put money into the card. At 1440p Ultra, modern games want about 11.5 GB, which the RTX 5070's 12 GB covers. At 4K Ultra they want about 13 GB, more than it has, so if a 4K monitor might come later, the 16 GB cards are the safer pick. At 4K with upscaling, every card here is still the limit: about 86 FPS for the 5070 and 102 for the 9070 XT.

For more on the trade-off, see our guides to the best CPU for the RTX 5070 and the best CPU for the RX 9070 XT.

When the Ryzen 5 7600X3D or 9600X is worth it

At native 1440p, a faster CPU adds little: the X3D build is only 4 FPS ahead of the Ryzen 5 7600 with the same RX 9070 XT. It starts to pay off in four situations.

  • You always use upscaling with a fast card. DLSS and FSR make the card faster, so the CPU has to keep up. With FSR Quality, the Ryzen 5 7600 becomes 20% CPU-limited with the RX 9070 XT at about 133 FPS. The Ryzen 5 9600X is 13% CPU-limited at 143, and the Ryzen 5 7600X3D is balanced at 153. With the RTX 5070 or RX 9070, the 7600 stays balanced even with upscaling, so the cheaper chip is fine there.
  • You play CPU-heavy games. In simulators, strategy games and MMOs at 1440p every build is CPU-limited. The X3D is about 20% faster than the 7600 (90 vs 75 FPS), and the 9600X about 9%.
  • You play esports on a 360 Hz monitor. Only the X3D build clears 360 FPS.
  • You care about 1% lows. The extra cache smooths frame times: in our model the X3D build holds 99 FPS 1% lows at 1440p against 89 for the 7600.

If none of those apply, the 7600 is the value pick and the money is better spent on the card. The Ryzen 5 9600X sits in between: a little faster, usually a little more expensive, and it needs its own cooler. The Ryzen 5 7600X3D has mainly been sold through Micro Center in the US, so availability varies; a Ryzen 7 7800X3D is the wider-available alternative at a higher price. See Ryzen 5 9600X vs Ryzen 5 7600, Ryzen 5 7600X3D vs Ryzen 7 7800X3D and our best AM5 CPU guide.

The upgrade path

An AM5 build with a 750 W supply leaves room for both main upgrades.

A faster graphics card

The Ryzen 5 7600 keeps up with an RTX 5080 at native 1440p (balanced, about 127 FPS). With DLSS Quality, though, it becomes 31% CPU-limited. So a card upgrade on its own works if you play native, and pairs best with a CPU upgrade if you rely on upscaling. Check the power figure first: NVIDIA recommends 850 W for an RTX 5080.

A faster processor

AM5 takes later Ryzen chips on the same board after a BIOS update. A Ryzen 7 9800X3D makes the RTX 5080 balanced with DLSS at 1440p, about 182 FPS. Our 9800X3D vs 7800X3D comparison covers which X3D chip is worth it.

Memory and storage

If you started with 16 GB, adding a second pair to reach 32 GB is the cheapest upgrade with a real effect, mainly fewer stutters in big open-world games. Add a second NVMe drive when the first fills up.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 1440p gaming PC cost in 2026?

About $1,200 with 16 GB of RAM, or $1,270–1,730 with 32 GB, for a Ryzen 5 7600 and RTX 5070 build at approximate October 2026 US street prices. Memory and graphics card prices rose through 2026, so check current listings.

What is the best graphics card for 1440p gaming on a budget?

The RTX 5070, RX 9070 or RX 9070 XT. With a Ryzen 5 7600 at 1440p High they average about 100, 104 and 117 FPS in AAA games. The RX 9070 cards have 16 GB of video memory; the RTX 5070 has 12 GB and stronger ray tracing.

Is the Ryzen 5 7600 good enough for 1440p gaming?

Yes. It keeps an RTX 5070, RX 9070 or RX 9070 XT GPU-limited at native 1440p. With upscaling, it stays balanced with the 5070 and 9070, but holds a 9070 XT back by about 20%.

Is the Ryzen 5 7600X3D worth it for 1440p?

Only in specific cases: CPU-heavy games, where it's about 20% faster than a Ryzen 5 7600, esports on a 360 Hz monitor, or a fast card with upscaling always on. At native 1440p in AAA games it adds only a few FPS, and the money usually buys more as a faster graphics card.

Is 12 GB of VRAM enough for 1440p?

For 1440p in 2026, yes. Modern games want about 11.5 GB at 1440p Ultra, which fits in the RTX 5070's 12 GB. At 4K Ultra they want about 13 GB, so a 16 GB card is safer if you might move to 4K.

What power supply do I need for a 1440p gaming PC?

A good 750 W unit covers the RTX 5070, RX 9070 and RX 9070 XT with headroom. NVIDIA recommends 650 W for an RTX 5070 and 850 W for an RTX 5080, so 750 W is a sensible middle if you might upgrade.
