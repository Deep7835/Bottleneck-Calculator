Best AM5 CPU for gaming in 2026

AM5 has a CPU for every graphics card, from a six-core Ryzen 5 that keeps an RTX 5070 busy at 1440p to the X3D chips that can feed an RTX 5090. The trick is not overspending on the processor for your card, while leaving room to drop in a faster one later. Every table here uses the same data as our bottleneck calculator.

Quick picks

Best value: Ryzen 5 7600. Balanced with an RTX 5070 at 1080p and with an RTX 5070 Ti at 1440p. The Ryzen 5 7500F is the same chip without integrated graphics, within 1% in games.

Best for most gamers: Ryzen 7 7800X3D. About 29% faster than the 7600 when the CPU is the limit, with better 1% lows and enough headroom for an RTX 5080.

Fastest: Ryzen 7 9800X3D. Another 14% over the 7800X3D, and the chip to buy for an RTX 5090 or a 360 Hz monitor. The newer 9850X3D adds only about 3%.

Gaming plus heavy work: Ryzen 9 9950X3D. Games like a 9800X3D and has 16 cores for rendering and compiling.

Skip for a gaming build with a real graphics card: the Ryzen 8000G APUs and the 8400F. An 8700G holds an RTX 5070 back by 20% at 1080p.

The AM5 gaming lineup, chip by chip

Ryzen 5 7500F and Ryzen 5 7600

Six Zen 4 cores and 32 MB of L3 cache. The 7500F drops the small integrated GPU and a sliver of clock speed, which makes no real difference with a graphics card installed. Either one is plenty for RTX 5060 to RTX 5070 class cards. See Ryzen 5 7600 vs Ryzen 5 7500F.

Ryzen 5 9600X and Ryzen 7 9700X

Zen 5 brings about 9% more gaming speed over the 7600 for the six-core 9600X, and the eight-core 9700X adds only 4% more on top in games. They're good chips, but at their usual prices an X3D part is the better gaming buy. Compare Ryzen 5 9600X vs Ryzen 5 7600 and Ryzen 7 9700X vs Ryzen 7 7800X3D before you pick one.

Ryzen 5 7600X3D and 7500X3D

Six-core X3D chips with 96 MB of L3. The 7600X3D is about 21% faster than a 7600 when CPU-bound and only 7% behind the 7800X3D, and the 7500X3D trails the 7600X3D by about 4%. Neither is sold everywhere, so availability and price vary a lot by country. See Ryzen 5 7600X3D vs Ryzen 7 7800X3D.

Ryzen 7 7800X3D

The chip that made X3D the default for gaming: eight cores, 96 MB of L3, modest power draw. It still keeps an RTX 5070 Ti or RX 9070 XT fully fed at 1080p, and it runs cool on a mid-range air cooler.

Ryzen 7 9800X3D and 9850X3D

The 9800X3D moves the cache underneath the CPU die, which lets it clock higher and be overclocked. It's about 14% ahead of the 7800X3D in our index. The 9850X3D is a higher-clocked version of the same design and only about 3% faster. Buy whichever costs less. The 9800X3D vs 7800X3D guide covers whether the upgrade is worth it, and Ryzen 7 9850X3D vs Ryzen 7 9800X3D has the newer pair.

Ryzen 9 9900X3D and 9950X3D

Two chiplets (eight cores each on the 9950X3D, six on the 9900X3D), with the extra cache on only one. AMD's chipset driver steers games onto the cache chiplet, so the 9950X3D performs like a 9800X3D, and the 9900X3D, with six cores under the cache, lands near a 7800X3D. They make sense when you also render, encode or compile. For gaming alone, the eight-core chips are better value. See Ryzen 9 9950X3D vs Ryzen 7 9800X3D and Ryzen 9 9900X3D vs Ryzen 7 9800X3D.

Ryzen 8000G and 8400F: a caution

The 8700G, 8600G and 8500G are laptop-derived APUs with strong integrated graphics, half the L3 cache of the 7600 and PCIe 4.0 instead of 5.0. They're great for a small PC with no graphics card. With one, they're slower than they look: the 8700G is behind even the 7500F in games, and with an RTX 5070 at 1080p the 8700G, 8600G and 8400F are 20%, 24% and 28% CPU-limited.

The X3D advantage: where the extra cache pays

AMD's X3D chips stack 64 MB of extra L3 cache onto a CPU chiplet. Games reuse the same world, AI and physics data every frame, and a bigger cache means fewer slow trips to memory. That's why the gain shows up where the CPU is under the most pressure:

  • 1% lows. With an RTX 5080 at 1440p, the 7800X3D's average is 9% higher than the 7600's in our model, but its 1% lows are 22% higher. Smoother frame pacing matters more than a few extra average FPS.
  • CPU-heavy games. In strategy, simulation and MMO-style games at 1440p with an RTX 5070 Ti, the 7600 manages about 75 FPS, the 7800X3D 97 and the 9800X3D 109. Every chip is still the limit there, which is why these games are the best case for X3D.
  • High-refresh esports. In CS2-style games at 1080p with an RTX 5070, the 7600 reaches about 349 FPS, the 9600X 375, the 7800X3D 422 and the 9800X3D 445. Pair a 9800X3D with an RTX 5080 and it's about 510.

In a GPU-bound build, such as most 1440p builds with an RTX 5070, the cache shows up in lows and CPU-heavy titles, not the average. The FPS calculator has per-game numbers.

The AM5 upgrade path: buy for today, drop in later

AMD has kept AM5 as its desktop socket since 2022 and has said it plans to support it through at least 2027. Ryzen 7000 and 9000 chips share the socket, so a cheap CPU now and a fast one later is a sensible plan.

Here's how it plays out. A Ryzen 5 7600 with an RTX 5070 at 1440p is 26% GPU-limited at 100 FPS. Keep that CPU and move to an RTX 5090, and it becomes 32% CPU-limited at 135 FPS. Drop in a 9800X3D on the same board and memory and it's balanced at 183 FPS, 35% more, with no rebuild.

That's the main reason to choose AM5 over a dead-end platform. On a 12th to 14th gen Intel board, see the LGA1700 upgrade guide first. On AM4, a 5700X3D games close to a 7600, so read the AM4 upgrade guide too.

Setup: BIOS, EXPO memory, board and cooler

  1. Check the BIOS version. Every AM5 board supports Ryzen 7000, but boards built before Ryzen 9000 arrived need a newer BIOS (a newer AMD AGESA) for 9000-series and 9000X3D chips. Check your board's CPU support list for the first BIOS that lists your chip. Many boards have BIOS Flashback, which updates from a USB stick with no CPU installed.
  2. Update even if it already boots. Early 2023 releases let some boards apply too much SoC voltage to X3D chips, which later updates corrected. Newer versions also improve memory compatibility and boot times.
  3. Enable EXPO. AM5 is DDR5-only. A 32 GB kit (two sticks) of DDR5-6000 CL30 is the usual sweet spot, because it keeps the memory controller running in sync with the memory. Without EXPO enabled, DDR5 runs at a slow default speed.
  4. Expect a long first boot. AM5 trains the memory on the first start and after BIOS changes, which can leave a black screen for a minute or more. Let it finish.
  5. Pick a sensible board. An A620 board is fine for a 65 W chip like the 7600 or 7500F. For an X3D or 12- and 16-core chip, a B650 or B850 board is the safer floor. You don't need X870E for gaming.
  6. Install AMD's chipset driver, especially for the 9900X3D and 9950X3D, which rely on it to keep games on the cache chiplet.

The X3D chips don't include a cooler, but a decent single-tower air cooler handles a 7800X3D or 9800X3D in games. Give the 12- and 16-core chips a bigger cooler if you render.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AM5 CPU for gaming?

The Ryzen 7 9800X3D is the fastest widely available choice, about 14% ahead of the 7800X3D when the CPU is the limit. The newer 9850X3D adds only about 3%. For most builds with an RTX 5070 Ti or RTX 5080, the Ryzen 7 7800X3D is the better-value pick.

Is the Ryzen 5 7600 good enough for gaming in 2026?

Yes, for most graphics cards. In our model it's balanced with an RTX 5070 at 1080p and with an RTX 5070 Ti at 1440p. It starts to hold back an RTX 5080 at 1080p and an RTX 5090 below 4K.

Is the Ryzen 5 9600X worth it over the 7600?

Only if the price gap is small. The 9600X is about 9% faster in CPU-bound games, which shows at 1080p high refresh. At 1440p with an RTX 5070 or 5070 Ti, the two land within a few FPS because the graphics card is the limit.

Should I buy the 9950X3D for gaming?

Only if you also do heavy multi-core work. In games it performs like a 9800X3D, because games run on the chiplet with the extra cache. For gaming alone, the eight-core 9800X3D costs less and is just as fast.

Are the Ryzen 8000G APUs good for gaming with a graphics card?

Not with a fast card. They have half the L3 cache of the Ryzen 5 7600 and slower PCIe. An 8700G holds an RTX 5070 back by about 20% at 1080p. They make sense in a small PC that uses the integrated graphics.

What RAM should I use with an AM5 CPU?

32 GB of DDR5 in two sticks, ideally DDR5-6000 CL30, with EXPO enabled in the BIOS. X3D chips are the least sensitive to memory speed, but without EXPO any AM5 chip runs memory at a slow default speed.
